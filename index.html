Classifying open problems in mathematics papers

Thank you for helping. We study how often mathematics papers pose open problems, and how well simple, transparent rules can find them. You will see the title, abstract and introduction of 120 arXiv papers. For each, tell us whether the paper itself poses or explicitly flags an open mathematical problem, and whether it solves (or makes progress on) a problem posed before.

Your answers are saved as you go. You can stop and come back later on the same computer and browser; to move to another computer, press Download a copy and use Import there.

About 2–4 minutes per paper. You do not need to finish all papers: every answer is useful.

What counts as posing an open problem

Answer yes if, in the abstract or introduction, the authors in their own voice:

  1. pose a new problem: they state their own conjecture or question (“we conjecture…”, “we ask whether…”, “Question 1.3”, “it would be interesting to know whether…”);
  2. flag a problem as open now: “… remains open”, “is still unknown”, “has not been proved”, “we were unable to …”, “the general case remains open”;
  3. list open problems: a survey or section that presents open problems.

Answer no when the paper only proves, disproves or makes progress on someone else's problem without saying what remains open; when a conjecture appears only as background; or when a “Conjecture” statement is someone else's conjecture that the paper then proves. Use unsure freely.

Second question: does the paper solve a known problem?

Independently of question 1, say whether the paper resolves (proves or disproves) a conjecture or problem posed earlier, makes partial progress on one (a special case, a better bound, a weaker form), or neither. A paper that only proves someone else's conjecture is “No” to question 1 and “Resolves it” to question 2; a paper can be “Yes” to both.

Keyboard: Y yes, N no, U unsure, → or J next, ← or K previous (not while typing). Use your browser's Ctrl+F to search the text; the box in the sidebar finds papers that contain a word.
